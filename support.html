Quick start

  1. Add the visual to your report from Microsoft AppSource.
  2. Drag your Latitude column into the Latitude field well — decimal degrees, e.g. 40.4168.
  3. Drag your Longitude column into the Longitude field well — e.g. -3.7038.
  4. Drop a numeric field or measure into Value. This is what colours each tile.
  5. In the Format pane, open Map Settings and pick your Country / Region.
The visual works from raw coordinates. There is no region-name or ISO-code lookup — each latitude/longitude pair is assigned to the grid cell that contains it. Coastal points that fall just off the land mask are snapped to the nearest land cell within 3 cells.

What's new in 1.3

  • Pick a range in the legend — click a class, rule or category, or drag across the gradient, and only the tiles in that range stay in full colour, with how many they are and their share of the total.

  • Label content — value, name, or name and value in two lines.

  • Highlighting from other visuals, on-object formatting, and twelve languages.
  • Numbers use your model's format; text shrinks with the visual; thinner borders on small tiles.

How it works

Every refresh follows the same steps, in this order.

1. Rows → coordinates

  • Each row needs a numeric Latitude and Longitude in decimal degrees (WGS84).
  • A row whose latitude or longitude is blank or not a number is skipped and counted as without coordinates.

2. Coordinates → tile

  • Each bundled map is a raster of land cells laid over a latitude/longitude bounding box. A point goes to the cell that contains it.
  • If that cell is sea (common for coastal cities), the point moves to the nearest land cell within 3 cells. Canarias and Hawaii use grids split 4×4, so they search 12 of their smaller cells: the same distance on the ground.
  • A point farther away than that is counted as outside the map.
  • Rows for other countries are not dropped silently: they count as outside the map. Filter them out (for example with a country slicer) when you only want one country.

4. Aggregation per tile

  • Auto (from field) reads the aggregation Power BI applies in the field well (Sum, Average, Min, Max or Count), from the field's query or its display name ("Average of …"). A model measure gives no hint, so it is treated as Sum. For ratios and averages written as measures, choose Average in Map Settings.
  • Sum, Average, Min and Max use only rows that have a value: blanks are ignored, not counted as zero. Count counts all rows in the tile.
  • The tooltip names the aggregation used and shows Rows: N (M with a value) when some rows are blank. Tooltip fields are aggregated with their own field-well aggregation.

5. Number format

  • Tiles, legend, colour-rule legend and tooltips use each measure's own format string from your model (currency, percentage, decimals) and the report's language, like the built-in visuals.
  • Large values get automatic units (K, M, bn). Percentages are never scaled.
  • Decimal Places in Map Settings applies once you set it; until then the measure's decimals are used. Tiles that count rows show whole numbers.

7. Style template

  • The first card of the format pane, as in the rest of the TCViz collection.
  • Report theme (free, the default): the colour ramp comes from the first data colour of your report theme, the diverging ends from its good/bad sentiment colours and the legend text from its secondary text colour. The visual has no background of its own.
  • Boardroom, Ledger, Editorial, Signal, Aurora and Terrace Pro: each sets the ramp, empty-tile colour, legend colour, border colour (visible with Show Cell Borders on), selection ring and the visual's background (dark in Boardroom and Aurora), with a small margin around the map. Ledger and Editorial also use a serif legend font.
  • A colour you set yourself always wins over the style. Choosing a style clears the saved colours it defines, so you see it as designed.

8. Target Pro

  • Drop a measure (budget, plan, last year) into Target, with your actuals in Value. Each tile is then coloured by its variance: (Value − Target) ÷ |Target|.
  • The scale is diverging and centred on 0%: below target, on target, above target. It is symmetric, so +10% and −10% weigh the same. Its ends are the 95th percentile of the variances, so one extreme tile does not wash out the rest: tiles beyond it take the full colour and the legend shows ≤ / ≥. The colours are the style's negative and positive colours (with Report theme, your theme's sentiment colours).
  • The legend and tile labels read in variance (+12.5%); colour rules compare against the variance, typed as shown (10 means +10%). The tooltip shows the actual value, the target and the variance.
  • A tile with no value, no target or a target of 0 has no variance and uses No Data Color.
  • Remove the Target field to colour by value again.

9. Legend — and picking a range

Pick a range: click a class, a rule or a category in the legend, or drag across the gradient. Only the tiles in that range stay in full colour, the rest dim, and a note under the map says how many tiles are in the range and what share of the total they add up to (the share is shown for Sum and Count). Click the same entry again, or click the gradient without dragging, to clear it. The range only affects the map: it does not filter the report and it is not saved with it. It is not available while formatting on the canvas.

Position (Top or Bottom), Font, Text Size (8–24) and Text Color are set in Format → Legend.

10. Size Pro

  • With a measure in Size, each tile's width and height become 30% + 70% × √(tile size ÷ largest tile size) of the full tile, so the area grows roughly in proportion to the size value.
  • Tiles with a blank or negative size are drawn at the 30% minimum.
  • Size is not applied to Custom TopoJSON regions.

11. Shape, labels and layout

  • Tile Shape: Square (Free), Circle or Hexagon (Pro). Hexagons shift every odd row by half a tile so they interlock.
  • Correct Latitude Distortion (on by default): a degree of longitude is shorter on the ground than a degree of latitude, by the cosine of the latitude. The tile height is set to cell height ÷ (cell width × cos(mid-latitude of the map)) times its width, limited to between 0.4 and 2.5, so countries like Norway are not stretched. Turn it off for square tiles.
  • The map is centred and scaled to fit the visual; if it is too small, the visual asks you to enlarge it. Below about 760×440 the legend, notes and start screen shrink with the visual (to 60%, never below 8 px).
  • Labels (Show Cell Labels) and Label Content:
    • Auto: when a tile is at least Label Min Tile Size pixels it shows the value; between half that size and the full size, the first 4 characters of its Label.
    • Value or Name: always that one. Names that do not fit are shortened with "…".
    • Name and value: the name above (slightly smaller) and the value below, like the tile maps in newspapers. When both lines do not fit, the value is kept (or the name, if the value does not fit).
    • The name is the tile's first Label. Labels are never smaller than 8 px (Label Font Size 8–14) and never wider than their tile: what does not fit is not drawn. Tile labels are not drawn on Custom TopoJSON regions.
  • Borders: Border Width (0.1–3) is a maximum. On small tiles the border thins with them (2% of the tile, never below 0.15 px) so it does not eat the colour. Custom TopoJSON regions keep a fixed 0.5 px white outline.

12. Interaction

  • Click a tile to filter the other visuals; the rest dim. Click the same tile again, or click the background, to clear.
  • Ctrl+click adds or removes tiles. Right-click opens the Power BI context menu.
  • Highlighting: click a bar or a slice in another visual and the map keeps every tile in place, showing the matching ones in full colour and dimming the rest, like the built-in visuals. It needs a measure in Value, Size, Target or Tooltips. A highlight that matches no tile dims the whole map.
  • Keyboard: arrow keys move focus, Enter / Space select, Ctrl+Enter adds to the selection, Esc clears, Shift+F10 or the context-menu key opens the context menu.
  • Selections from bookmarks and other visuals are shown on the tiles.
  • Tooltip: region name (TopoJSON), up to 3 labels (+N more), the aggregated value (with Target: actual, target and variance), Size, Category (+N more), the colour rule that coloured the tile (when rules are on), row count, cell centre coordinates and your tooltip fields.
  • Report page tooltips are supported: set a tooltip page in Format → General → Tooltips, and it is filtered to the rows of the tile under the pointer.
  • Where the report does not allow interaction, clicks, the legend range and the keyboard selection are off.

13. On-object formatting

  • Turn on On-object interaction in Power BI Desktop (Options → Preview features), then select the tiles, the tile labels or the legend on the canvas.
  • Tiles: tile shape, labels, empty cells, borders, empty-tile colour and border colour. Labels: label size. Legend: position, font, size and colour. Tiles and labels link to the Map Settings card, the legend to the Legend card.
  • While formatting, a click selects what to format and does not filter the report.

14. Custom TopoJSON Pro

  • Choose Custom TopoJSON (Pro) in Country / Region and drag a file onto the visual. The file is read locally; no network request is made.
  • From the topology, the object with the most geometries is used. Each Polygon or MultiPolygon geometry is one region (holes respected).
  • Region names come from the properties name, NAME, NAME_1, name_en, NAME_EN, or the geometry id; otherwise "Region N".
  • Maximum 5 MB. Coordinates must be longitude/latitude (WGS84); projected files are detected and a note explains it. Files that are not TopoJSON or have no polygons are explained too.
  • The file is stored in the report (.pbix). Save the report after dropping it.

15. Pro preview and licensing

  • Without a licence, a Pro option you turn on while editing is drawn working, under a "Pro preview" notice that names it — you see your own data as hexagons, on a diverging scale or against your target, not a screenshot of someone else's.
  • Power BI shows its own licence notice: a banner naming the Pro features in use, then the licence icon with the purchase path.
  • In reading view, and wherever the licence cannot be read (Publish to Web, embedded reports, exports), the free result is shown with no notice, so a published report never uses a feature nobody paid for.
  • Custom TopoJSON without a licence: while editing, the drop zone appears and the regions are drawn under the preview notice; in reading view the visual shows "Custom TopoJSON regions are not available".
  • The 30-day trial and the payment grace period count as Pro.

Custom TopoJSON Pro

  1. Set Map Settings → Country / Region to Custom TopoJSON (Pro).
  2. Drag a .json or .topojson file directly onto the visual. The drop zone appears automatically.
  3. Rows are assigned to regions by point-in-polygon testing and coloured by the aggregated value. A MultiPolygon region counts as one region, and the tooltip shows the region name.
  4. The file is stored inside the .pbix, so it survives closing and reopening the report. Remember to save after dropping it.

File requirements

  • A valid TopoJSON topology with Polygon or MultiPolygon geometries.
  • Coordinates in longitude/latitude (WGS84). Files in projected coordinates are detected and the visual explains that they need to be exported in WGS84.
  • Maximum 5 MB. Invalid or oversized files are rejected with an explanation.

Files are loaded by drag & drop only. The visual makes no network requests, so there is no option to load a file from a URL.

Where to find boundary files

  • geoBoundaries.org — administrative divisions worldwide
  • Natural Earth — country, state and province outlines
  • Your national statistics office — most publish TopoJSON or GeoJSON

Tip: simplify large files at mapshaper.org to 5–10% before loading. The shape stays the same, the file fits the 5 MB limit and rendering is faster.

Tips & hints

Plan vs actual on a map

Put actuals in Value and the budget in Target. Tiles below plan take the negative colour of your theme (red in most); switch Label Content to Name and value and the variance reads on each tile.

"Where are we above 500 K?"

Drag across the right end of the legend gradient. The note under the map tells you how many tiles that is and what share of the total they carry — useful before a meeting, without building another visual.

One outlier washes out the map

Switch the scale to Quantile (Pro): five classes with the same number of tiles each, so the rest of the country is readable again. Click a class in the legend to see which tiles fall in it.

Small tile on a busy page

Keep Report theme so the map matches the other visuals, turn on Show Cell Borders with Border Width 0.5 — it thins itself on small tiles — and let the text shrink with the visual.

A drill-down without building one

Create a report page tooltip with a small table or chart and set it in Format → General → Tooltips. Hovering a tile shows that page filtered to the tile.

Name the tiles

Put a short name in Label (a province code, a store number). With Name or Name and value the map reads like a newspaper tile map; long names are shortened with "…".

Coastal cities missing

Points in the sea snap to the nearest land cell within 3 cells. If a city is still outside, check that Latitude and Longitude are not swapped — the note under the map counts those rows.

FAQ

Rows are reported as "outside the map" (or "No rows fall inside …").

Those coordinates are more than 3 cells away from any land cell of the selected map. Check that Country / Region matches your data, that Latitude and Longitude are not swapped, and filter out other countries with a slicer — rows from other countries always count as outside.

Where do I turn on the Target mode?

There is no switch: drop a measure into the Target field well (with your actuals in Value). Remove it to colour by value again. Without a licence it is drawn under the Pro preview notice.

Most of the map is dimmed.

Either a range is picked in the legend (the note under the map says so — click the legend entry again, or click the gradient, to clear it), a tile is selected (click the background), or another visual is highlighting the map (clear the selection in that visual).

All tiles have the same colour, or my colour rules don't apply.

Check the Enable Rules switch in the Conditional Formatting card header. Rules are tested 1 → 2 → 3 and the first match wins, so a wide Rule 2 range can hide Rule 3. For a percent field type 25, not 0.25. With high contrast on, rules are not used. If every tile has the same value, the scale has nothing to spread.

The legend is missing.

Check Show Legend. No legend is drawn when no tile has a value, or with the Categorical scale and no Category field. A very small visual may leave no room.

An average looks like a sum.

A model measure is aggregated as Sum by default. Set Value Aggregation to Average in Map Settings; the tooltip shows which aggregation is used.

Labels are not showing.

Turn on Show Cell Labels. A label is only drawn when it fits at 8 px or more: enlarge the visual, reduce Decimal Places, lower Label Min Tile Size, or choose Name in Label Content. Small grids such as Spain need a fairly large visual.

My TopoJSON is not loading.

You need Pro (or the Pro preview while editing) and Custom TopoJSON (Pro) selected before dropping. The file must be a TopoJSON topology (not GeoJSON) with polygons, up to 5 MB, in longitude/latitude. Read the note under the map: it says which of these failed.

What coordinate format does it expect?

Decimal degrees in WGS84 — 40.4168, not 40°25'00"N. Convert DMS values before loading.

Some tiles are empty.

Those grid cells have no matching coordinates. Turn off Show Empty Cells in Map Settings to hide them.

I see "Showing the first 500 of N rows".

Expected on the Free tier, which uses the first 500 rows. Aggregate your data upstream, or use Pro for up to 30,000 rows.

Values don't match my card visual.

Check the aggregation named in the tooltip. With Auto (from field) the visual follows the field-well aggregation; set Value Aggregation explicitly in Map Settings to match the card.

I bought Pro but the features are still locked.

Sign out of Power BI Desktop and sign back in to refresh your licence status. The visual reads the licence after the first render and repaints with all rows once it arrives, so give it a moment.

My dropped TopoJSON disappeared after reopening the report.

It shouldn't — the file is persisted into the .pbix. Make sure you saved the report after dropping the file.

Does the visual send my data anywhere?

No. The visual makes no network requests at all: no telemetry, no analytics, no cookies, and your data never leaves the Power BI environment. See the Privacy Policy.

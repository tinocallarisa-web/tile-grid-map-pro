## Description

```
On a filled map, every province gets one colour, however different the towns inside it are. A busy coast and an empty interior read as one average, and the largest regions take the eye whether or not anything happens there. You are reading boundaries, not data.

A tile grid map lays the country out as a grid of equal tiles and colours each tile by the rows that fall in it. Where your outlets, sales or customers actually are shows up tile by tile, at the same resolution everywhere, in a shape you still recognise — with no boundary files, no region codes and no map service.

TWENTY-SEVEN GRIDS, READY TO USE

Spain (and the Canaries separately), Portugal, France, Germany, Italy (plus Sicily and Sardinia), the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Belgium, Poland, Sweden, Norway, Switzerland, Austria, the United States (plus Alaska and Hawaii), Canada, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, Australia, India and Japan.

Bind latitude, longitude and a value. Each row snaps to its nearest tile — no region codes to look up, no join to maintain, no name spelling to get right.

READING THE MAP

• Pick a range in the legend: click a class or drag across the colour bar, and only the tiles in that range stay lit, with how many they are and their share of the total
• Aggregation per tile: automatic, sum, average, count, minimum or maximum
• Up to three colour rules with their own legend, for thresholds that mean something to your business
• Labels on the tiles: the value, the name, or both on two lines, like a newspaper tile map
• Your measure's own number format, and colours that follow the report theme
• Tooltips with up to ten fields, or a report page as the tooltip
• Correct latitude distortion, so northern countries are no longer stretched

PRO

• Target: put the budget next to the actuals and every tile shows how far it is from plan, below or above, on a scale centred on zero
• Up to 30,000 rows instead of the first 500
• Six collection styles, from a dark boardroom look to a printed ledger
• Circle and hexagon tiles as well as squares
• Your own colours on the sequential scale
• Quantile, diverging and categorical scales — diverging around zero for gains, losses and variances, categorical to colour by a category field rather than by value
• A Size field: the tile keeps its place in the grid and changes area, so you can read two measures at once
• Custom TopoJSON: drop in your own regions — sales territories, health districts, franchise areas — and they are saved inside the report

SEE IT BEFORE YOU BUY IT

Turn on a Pro feature without a licence and it is drawn working, under a "Pro preview" watermark that names it. You see your own data as a hexagon grid, on a diverging scale, sized by a second measure — not a screenshot of someone else's data. Reading view shows the free result with no watermark, so a published report never uses a feature nobody paid for.

NATIVE INTEGRATION

• Click a tile to cross-filter the report; Ctrl+click for several
• Click another visual and the map highlights the matching tiles instead of losing the rest
• Format the tiles, labels and legend directly on the canvas
• Right-click for the Power BI context menu
• Keyboard navigation, high contrast, and twelve languages

PRIVACY

The visual makes no network requests of any kind: no map tile server, no geocoding service, no analytics, no telemetry. Your coordinates never leave the report. That is also why the grids ship inside the visual and why custom regions arrive by drag and drop rather than from a URL.

GETTING STARTED

1. Add the visual and bind Latitude, Longitude and a Value.
2. Pick your country under Format → Map Settings.
3. Pro: add a Target or a Size measure, or drop in your own TopoJSON for territories that are not a country.

Documentation: https://tinocallarisa-web.github.io/tile-grid-map-pro/support.html
Support: support@tcviz.com

WHAT'S NEW IN 1.3.0.0

Target (Pro), picking a range in the legend, names on the tiles, the report theme and six styles, highlighting from other visuals, on-object formatting, and the pane and messages in twelve languages.
```

## Search keywords (max 3)

```
tile grid map
cartogram
choropleth alternative
```

`tile grid map` is the name of the technique and what someone who already knows they want one
will type. `cartogram` is the academic term for the same idea and brings the people who know
the concept but not this name for it. `choropleth alternative` catches the ones who have a
normal filled map that is not working and are looking for a way out — that is the moment
this visual is bought.

`map` alone was not used: it competes with Azure Maps, Bing and every filled map in the
marketplace, and it brings people looking for roads and pins, which this visual deliberately
does not do.
